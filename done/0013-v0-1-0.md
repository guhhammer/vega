# 0013 — Preparing v0.1.0

`docs: prepare the v0.1.0 release`

## What landed

`.documentation/releasing.md`, the changelog cut from *Unreleased* to
`[0.1.0]`, and the tag.

## What the release actually claims

Very little, on purpose. The release body and the changelog both open by saying
nothing here has been audited, and the *Known limitations* section is placed
where a reader hits it before deciding whether to trust the thing.

The tempting alternative — a confident announcement listing the cryptography —
would be worse. Every one of those primitives is standard and none of them are
the risk. The risk is the ~6,000 lines around them that no third party has read,
and a release note that obscures that is doing marketing rather than
engineering.

## The wire format is not stable, and that is at the top

Not a footnote. Two different 0.x builds may not talk to each other, and commit
`0011` already changed the canonical encoding once — signatures and sealed boxes
from before it do not verify after it.

`releasing.md` names the four things that break compatibility with stored data
as well as with peers: the canonical encoding, the sealed-box construction, any
key derivation, and the sigchain entry format. Until 1.0 those get a minor bump
and a loud entry; after 1.0 they need a migration.

## What 1.0 would have to mean

Written down so it does not drift into "whenever it feels ready": the wire
format is stable, and someone other than the author has reviewed the
cryptography. Neither is true and neither is close.

Saying that in the repository is more useful than a version number that implies
otherwise. A 0.x that lists what it does not do is a more honest artifact than a
1.0 that lists what it does.

## Checklist over habit

`releasing.md` starts with `./make clean && ./make check` — from cold, because
the previous release preparation shipped a commit whose lints only failed on a
clean build. The incremental cache had hidden them. That happened once and the
checklist now makes it hard to repeat.
